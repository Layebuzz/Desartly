# Understand the product before writing

Build a concise working analysis from the project assets and the owner's context before selecting the story and taxonomy. Identify the actual product or service, rather than treating the project as a generic collection of colours and mockups. Apply this to branding and communication work as well as UI/UX; a physical product does not automatically belong to the CMS Product discipline.

The owner's descriptions, claims and interpretations are accepted project context. Incorporate them directly without asking the owner to prove them or repeatedly calling them probable. Independently read visible product names, model numbers, packaging copy, controls and usage scenes to make the analysis specific. Record sources privately; the public story should read naturally, without a disclaimer after every observation.

Cover the following where relevant, combining points when the project is small:

- **Product and value:** Name the product, model and functions. Explain the practical need it addresses and how it connects the user's intention to an action. Distinguish the displayed product from a wider brand offering when that distinction matters.
- **Industry and position:** Identify the actual market and relevant intersections, then formulate a concrete positioning sentence. Select the closest approved CMS industry; express the richer positioning in prose rather than creating taxonomy labels.
- **Audience:** Identify useful audience groups through their tasks, motivations and context. Explain the shared need. Use owner-provided audience information directly; do not invent demographic precision from a visual style.
- **Visual language:** Connect colour, material appearance, lettering, imagery, layout and technology cues to the brand's intended feeling and practical role. Explain how the choices work together, rather than merely listing them.
- **Brand personality:** Use Aaker's five dimensions—Sincerity, Excitement, Competence, Sophistication and Ruggedness—to articulate the visible design intent. A compact qualitative comparison can show a primary dimension and supporting dimensions. Map the primary dimension to the live CMS enum. This is a design interpretation; numerical scores or consumer-study results require actual supplied data.
- **Voice:** Derive a clear tone and, when useful, one short example line from the positioning and personality.

Use confident, concrete sentences. Avoid a chain of “possibly,” “likely,” “appears to” or caveats that obscures a well-supported conclusion. Ask only when an unresolved fact would change the product identity, commissioning, classification or central story. Do not add unprovided test results, measured impact, launch history or certifications. A natural material appearance alone is not evidence of environmental certification.

## TerraRoots example

With the owner's supplied project context, identify **TR-S1 as a soil sensor** measuring moisture, temperature and nutrients. “Local Soil Sensors & Bio-Fertilization” places the brand across soil monitoring and biological plant nutrition; the image shows the sensor. Its value is helping growers understand plant needs and make better-informed watering and soil-care decisions.

Position TerraRoots at the intersection of agricultural technology, gardening tools and plant care. Its audience includes home and urban gardeners, small local growers and people who care about soil health and resource use. A useful positioning sentence is: “Understandable tools for local growers who want to know their soil and care for plants with confidence.” Use Agriculture as the approved industry and Branding when the delivered work is the identity, packaging and applications.

Explain how brown, cream, terracotta and olive tones, kraft-like surfaces, wood and warm light ground the digital sensor in a familiar gardening context. Rounded lettering supports approachability; the display adds competence; local-growth messages express community. The primary personality is **Sincerity**, supported by **Competence**, with an outdoors-oriented Ruggedness accent. A suitable voice is warm, clear and practical: “Get to know your soil before you water.”

Use this example to guide the depth and connections of the analysis, not to copy TerraRoots facts or classifications into unrelated projects. Feed the analysis into the opening, challenge, design decisions and captions; do not force the entire analysis table into every public case study.
