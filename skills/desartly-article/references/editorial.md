# Voice and evidence

The owner’s approved captions are the strongest voice samples. Read two or three relevant examples once, only the announcement fields or supplied excerpts. Note everyday verbs, personal perspective, paragraph rhythm and cultural context. Write an article with the same person’s voice but enough precision and depth for its reader.

Persian: natural contemporary language, consistent نیم‌فاصله and punctuation, readable short-to-medium paragraphs. Avoid bureaucratic phrasing, exaggerated intimacy and slang added for effect. English: idiomatic, direct prose rather than literal Persian syntax. Keep technical terms consistent and explain them where useful.

First person may describe confirmed work or a clearly framed design judgment. Never manufacture memories, emotions, mistakes, conversations or opinions on the owner’s behalf. Separate what a source demonstrates from what the author infers. Mark hypothetical examples as hypothetical. Quotes require an exact verified source and compliance with quotation limits.

Give each section a job. Develop an argument through concrete examples, comparisons or annotated project visuals. Explain limitations when they affect the conclusion. Avoid stock openings, grand claims, decorative triads, repetitive “not X but Y,” forced FAQs and a conclusion that repeats every heading. Do not flatten a distinctive phrase merely because it is unusual.

Before delivery, check factual provenance, coherent argument, fluent language and consistency with the owner’s samples. Keep research notes private; place reader-facing source links beside supported claims. Preserve existing private announcement notes when revising. Captions summarize the article’s real insight and invite reading; English conveys the same claims as Persian. Hashtags are relevant social tags, not CMS taxonomy additions.
