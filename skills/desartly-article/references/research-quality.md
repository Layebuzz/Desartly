# Research, statistics and Google content quality

## Evidence worth using

For each article, identify the factual questions on which the argument depends. Search a bounded set of primary sources: original research papers, official datasets, standards, government/institution reports or transparent first-party studies. Prefer evidence relevant to the topic, population and timeframe over a fashionable but unrelated number. Read methodology and result context, not just the abstract or a search snippet. A commercial survey can be used with clear attribution and sampling limitations; do not present marketing claims as independent research.

Keep a private evidence ledger with claim, exact source URL/DOI, publisher/authors, publication date, access date, sample/population/geography, study period, method, reported value/unit and caveats. Record only information the source actually supplies; missing sample or methodology is a limitation. Explain what each important number supports and what it cannot establish. Distinguish correlation from causation, relative from absolute change, survey responses from behavior, lab results from field outcomes, and measured project results from external benchmarks. Keep translated numbers and qualifications consistent across languages.

Place source links beside the supported claims and preserve references in the CMS. Label derived calculations and show their inputs/formula. Do not pool incompatible studies, infer a trend from different populations or present an old figure as current. A chart requires traceable data, units, readable labels and honest scale; do not generate illustrative data that looks like research. Prefer a few useful findings to a stack of citations. No arbitrary minimum count of statistics or sources: sufficient evidence depends on the claims. If credible quantitative evidence is unavailable, state that gap and use verified qualitative evidence rather than fabricating precision.

## Google Search Central baseline

Official pages checked on 2026-10-09; revisit relevant guidance when carrying out a new article task if freshness matters:

- Helpful, reliable, people-first content: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content
- SEO Starter Guide: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide

Use these as self-assessment guidance, not a certification checklist. The article should answer its intended reader’s question fully, contribute original analysis beyond summarizing sources, use an accurate descriptive title, and make authorship and sourcing clear. Experience and expertise must be real; do not fabricate credentials or first-hand testing. Explain research/production methods where relevant to trust. Disclose substantial automation assistance when readers would reasonably need that context, without making false claims of unaided authorship.

Google does not prescribe an ideal article word count. E-E-A-T is not a single measurable ranking factor, and rater evaluations do not directly determine rankings. Never invent an E-E-A-T score, guarantee a position or label an article “Google approved.” Do not refresh the displayed date without substantive changes. SEO supports useful content: natural terminology, navigable structure, descriptive links, real sources and a good page experience. Metadata, crawl/indexing, canonical, schema and performance require actual website verification; unsupported site-level work belongs in the task list.

## CMS completeness and separate editorial review

- Foundation 6: specific title, explanatory excerpt, relevant cover, accurate author, actual article date, existing editorial category.
- Story 4: purposeful opening, developed chapters, scannable headings and a useful closing.
- Visuals & context 5: meaningful supporting images, alt descriptions, explanatory captions, checked sources and a real related project when applicable.
- Distribution 5: correct language, topic tags, private Persian caption, English caption and hashtags.

Read the live review because these definitions may evolve. Do not invent a related project or irrelevant illustration to reach 20/20. Separately verify the argument, source provenance, statistical interpretation, original contribution, fluent voice and intent match. Fix unsupported claims before publication. Report genuinely inapplicable checklist items and technical limitations accurately; a full score is not proof of quality or rankings.
